Solar Finance

CAPEX, RESCO, PPA or financed: choosing the right solar model

The panels are the same. How you pay for them changes your cash flow, your risk and who owns the asset.

13 November 2026 · 6 min read · Stellar Grid engineering team

Most solar conversations start with panels and inverters. The better ones start with a question: who pays, who owns, and who carries the performance risk? In India, four commercial structures cover almost every rooftop and ground-mount project. Here is how they compare.

1. CAPEX: you buy the plant

You pay the full project cost, own the plant from day one and keep every unit it generates. This gives the lowest lifetime cost of power and the fastest payback, but it needs capital upfront and you are responsible for O&M (which you can contract out through an AMC).

Best for: businesses and homeowners with available capital, a long-term view of the premises and an appetite to own an asset.

2. Financed CAPEX: you buy it with a loan

The same ownership as CAPEX, but the project is funded with a mix of equity and debt. A common split is 25% equity and 75% debt. The EMI is often lower than the electricity bill it replaces, so the plant can be cash-flow positive from the first month.

3. RESCO / PPA: you buy the power, not the plant

A developer builds, owns and maintains the plant on your roof or land. You sign a power purchase agreement (PPA) and pay an agreed tariff for every unit consumed, usually well below the grid tariff, for a fixed term. There is little or no upfront investment, and generation risk stays with the developer.

Best for: organisations that want savings without capital expenditure, or that prefer to keep solar off the balance sheet.

4. Open access: solar from somewhere else

If your roof is too small or your load is large, open access lets you buy solar power from an off-site plant through the grid. Feasibility depends on your connection voltage, state regulations and the charges applicable in your state, so it needs a careful commercial comparison against your current tariff.

A worked example

Take an industrial consumer in Rajasthan with a ₹1,50,000 monthly bill at a grid tariff of ₹8.30 per kWh. Using the same assumptions as our savings calculator (1,300 kWh per kW per year, 1% yearly degradation, O&M at 2% of project cost, 5% yearly tariff and O&M escalation):

Financing reduces the upfront payment by three quarters while giving up only a small share of the 20-year savings, because the EMI is comfortably covered by what the plant saves each month.

How to choose

  • Lowest lifetime cost: CAPEX.
  • Lowest upfront cost, still owning the asset: financed CAPEX.
  • No investment, no O&M responsibility: RESCO / PPA.
  • Large load, limited roof: open access, subject to state rules.
